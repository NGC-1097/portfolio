Math, Data and Computing is the area where I'm most aware of having a gap, and one I have to close because my stated direction is embedded systems and silicon-level work. The honest version of year 2 is that I failed both Math and Data Analysis. The overload from working at Picnic, team lead training, PRISM running in parallel, and two simultaneous CBLs caught up with me in exactly the subjects I needed most. I have to retake both in year 3, and that's not an excuse, it's central to how this expertise area looks right now.

There is real computing work in year 2 that doesn't show up in those courses though. The PRISM software UI/UX involves writing code at a non-trivial level. The infrastructure work is Linux system administration at a real level: Coolify configuration, self-hosted GitLab, Traefik with automatic SSL, working out the right way to mount storage, troubleshooting Docker conflicts. None of that is "math" but it is computing in the practical sense.

Year 1 was lighter. I had basic data analysis exposure and stated an intent to use Python tooling like pandas and SeaBorn for design-related data work, but execution didn't really go beyond stating it.
The forward plan is the only honest version: retake math and data analysis in year 3, take pre-master electives that build the math foundation for the embedded systems master, then use that to support eventual custom MCU work with open-source IP cores. 

#### SMART goal: pass math and data analysis retakes in year 3 with at least a 6 each, and pass at least one quantitative pre-master elective relevant to embedded systems by end of year 3.
