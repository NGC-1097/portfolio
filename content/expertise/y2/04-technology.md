Technology and Realization is the foundation I bring to every project. Before TU/e I had extensive C programming experience and Linux system management from working with embedded boards and electronics outside any formal program. In year 1 this translated into ESP32 and Processing work on game systems **[Melodoodle](/projects/melodoodle)** paper bridge construction in CBL Project 2 where I worked through truss design and load calculations, and Creative Electronics modules.

Year 2 is where this expertise was broadened significantly. On PRISM I'm responsible for the full technical stack: I designed the 3D model of the device, assembled the hardware, wrote the software UI/UX, and manage the entire self-hosted infrastructure myself. The infrastructure piece was a real learning moment. After hitting persistent Docker storage conflicts on the SURF Research Cloud VM I escalated to the university manager for a new VM with a larger root partition, then implemented proper Linux bind mounts via /etc/fstab rather than symlinks, which is what the SURF developers actually recommend. On CBL4 (Multi-Disciplinary CBL "Plasmas for a Better World") I'm on the engineering side of an ion wind thruster, working through architectures from wire-to-foil to multi-stage ducted variants. CBL3 Future Mobility involves the technical input on the interior and exterior design work, and the Aesthetics of Interaction course and Design Connected Experiences elective were both required to build actual prototypes.-

The trajectory from here is clear. I plan to move into an embedded systems master degree after the bachelor and am taking pre-master electives in year 3 to bridge the gap. The longer arc is custom MCU design using open-source IP for my keyboard startup and another big project in the making I cannot disclose as of now. 

#### SMART goal: pass the embedded electronics pre-master elective in year 3 with at least a 6.
