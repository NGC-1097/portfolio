User and Society is honestly my second-weakest expertise area, and being clear about that is the first step in addressing it. In year 1 I stated the intent to do ethnographic interviews and Wizard of Oz tests, but in practice the execution was limited. The methodology was discussed but not exercised at scale.

Year 2 added real material even if not all of it is formal user research. Aesthetics of Interaction is implicitly user-centered work, because the entire premise is observing how someone interacts with an artifact without scaffolding, although officially being part of the Creativity and Aesthetics EA. CBL3 Future Mobility involves user-facing design decisions for vehicle interiors, where assumptions about target users have direct consequences. The team lead role at Picnic is also relevant here in a less academic sense: I deal daily with a wide range of employees, including disrespectful and confused ones, and conflict mediation has been part of the training. That's real social-context exposure even if it doesn't fit the standard research methodology framing.

The bigger user research opportunity is sitting right in front of me. The friends and coworkers asking me weekly when the keyboard prototype will be done are an early-user panel I should be interviewing, not just shipping to. Same case for PRISM: the relevant stakeholders are researchers, civil authorities, and at-risk communities, and a proper stakeholder analysis is still pending.
Future: structured user research for the keyboard startup before launch, formal stakeholder analysis for PRISM, and the no-instruction testing format applied across products. 

#### SMART goal: conduct structured user research interviews with at least 5 potential keyboard users by end of year 3.
