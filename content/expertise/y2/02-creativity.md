Creativity and Aesthetics is where I had to genuinely stretch in year 2. In year 1 my exposure was more methodological: the Magic Machine Workshop in CBL2 taught me an "act before thought" approach to quick experimental prototyping, and I learned the basics of using different creativity techniques in idea generation. My aesthetic position was already defined, drawn from Jony Ive and Dieter Rams' "weniger, aber besser," but mostly as a philosophy I admired rather than something I'd practiced rigorously.

Year 2 turned this into actual practice. Aesthetics of Interaction was the most challenging elective I took, and probably the most useful. The brief was to design an alarm purely from the interaction perspective, with no instructions to the user, no preconceived form factor, no guidance from the professor about what the interaction should look like. I came in predefined on what an alarm "had to" be (hours, a way to set one) and had to let that go. What we ended up with was a buzzer that pulls the user out of doomscrolling, not a traditional alarm at all. Design Connected Experiences in the same quarter worked the other direction: gathering separate modules and making them behave as one experience, which is directly relevant to the keyboard startup. On PRISM I made the actual aesthetic decisions for the device enclosure (it has to look like a real product, not a hobbyist build, while surviving harsh climate conditions) and for the software UI/UX. CBL3 Future Mobility is the heaviest CA project of the year, since vehicle interior and exterior design is the brief itself aiming to create a 1:1 scale of our 3D model.

Where this goes: the no-instruction testing approach from Aesthetics of Interaction is exactly how I want to validate the keyboard modules with users, and I'll apply it to other products in the pipeline. I also want to formally document a visual and interaction design language for the keyboard startup, building on the Rams influence into something specific to my own products. 

#### SMART goal: run at least 3 no-instruction usability tests on keyboard prototypes with friends and coworkers by end of summer 2026.
