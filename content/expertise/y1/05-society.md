To me, User & Society is all about understanding people to make better designs. My studies, especially 'Human-Centered Design,' has taught me really useful ways to do this, like the Wizard of Oz technique to test ideas early, and how valuable quick, low-fi prototypes are for feedback.

Doing user studies is now a core part of how I design. I learned you have to think carefully about how you run these studies, not just collecting data, but also being ethical about privacy and respecting people. It made me realize the responsibility we have as designers and the concerns people might have with their information.

This is crucial to me personally, as I'm actively working on launching my own startup. The plan is to incorporate the company this June (2025) and dedicate the summer to heavy prototyping. To make sure we're building the right thing, I aim to run detailed usability tests with at least 10 potential users during this summer prototyping phase. The goal is then to release the first iteration or start building serious hype around Christmas 2025. Alongside this intense practical work, I also want to spend some time researching and writing a short piece by December about adapting user research for different cultural and socio-economic contexts, to keep broadening my perspective.
